Candidate Interview Prep Guide

Thank you for taking the time to interview with Beacon Software. This guide covers what to expect at each stage and how to prepare. The process is also an opportunity for you to learn about the work, meet our engineers, and decide whether Beacon is the right place for you.

About Beacon Software+
  • A permanent home for essential businesses. Beacon acquires and grows mission-critical software and services businesses that support the everyday economy. We invest for the long term rather than buying businesses to resell them.
  • Technology applied to real operating problems. Our teams bring engineering, AI, and operating expertise to help portfolio companies improve their products, serve customers, and grow.
  • Local expertise, shared capabilities. Portfolio companies retain their own identity and customer relationships while benefiting from Beacon’s shared resources and technology.
  • Engineers close to the business. Forward Deployed Engineers work directly with portfolio-company leaders and operators to understand their workflows, identify high-impact problems, and ship solutions. The role includes operating what you build and bringing reusable approaches back to the broader engineering team.

Our values: the four H’s+

Beacon’s values guide how we make decisions, work with each other, and build relationships with our portfolio companies.

  • Humility. We hold strong beliefs weakly, seek new ideas, and believe we can learn from anyone. Getting to the right answer often means being wrong along the way. Reflect on: a time feedback or evidence changed your approach, or an operator’s expertise helped you build a better solution.
  • Honesty. We seek the truth and build trust through integrity. Relationships matter over the long term, and trust requires consistent care. Reflect on: how you communicated a mistake, surfaced a risk, or set realistic expectations when the answer was uncertain. In technical discussions, distinguish what you verified from what you assume.
  • Hunger. We play to win, hold ourselves to high standards, and take responsibility for our teammates, partners, and customers. Reflect on: a difficult problem you took ownership of, how you maintained quality, and how you followed through to a meaningful result. For the take-home, demonstrate that judgment within the stated 2–4-hour window.
  • Horizon. We are building a generational company and take a long-term view, allowing improvements and relationships to compound. Reflect on: a decision where you balanced an immediate need with lasting value—for example, investing in reliability, maintainability, customer trust, or an approach others could reuse.

To prepare, choose a few genuine examples from your work and consider what you did, why, what happened, and what you learned. These prompts are reflection aids, not a list of promised interview questions; there is no need to memorize the values or script an answer for each one.

Your interviews

01Technical screen30 minutes · With one of our engineers+

What to expect

You’ll discuss something you personally owned and shipped to production. Choose an area where you can speak in depth about the problem, your contribution, the implementation, and what happened after launch. Your interviewer will ask follow-up questions about technical decisions, tradeoffs, and alternatives to understand your depth in an area of your choosing.

You will not be asked to show code. Please do not prepare slides or presentation materials. This is a fluid conversation, not a formal presentation, and it may go in several directions.

How to prepare

  • Pick one project you know well. Specific ownership and a clear understanding of the system matter more than choosing the biggest or most impressive project.
  • Practice a brief verbal introduction: who needed it, what problem it solved, what you owned, and what shipped. Leave room for questions rather than rehearsing a long monologue.
  • Refresh the architecture, key dependencies, constraints, and one or two difficult decisions. Be ready to explain alternatives you considered and why you chose your approach.
  • Think about production realities: testing, rollout, monitoring, failures, and evidence that the solution worked. Reflect on what you would change today.
  • Separate your own work from the team’s contributions. Keep employer and customer information confidential; anonymized examples are fine.

02Take-home project2–4 hours · Hands-on, AI-assisted engineering+

What to expect

You’ll receive a provisioned GitHub profile and access to the codebase of a fictional company. The application contains errors, bugs, and performance issues. Your task is to investigate and address those issues using whichever AI tooling you feel comfortable with.

AI use is part of this exercise. You should still understand the changes you make and be able to explain how you checked that they work.

How to prepare

  • Get comfortable using your preferred AI tools to navigate an unfamiliar codebase, investigate failures, make changes, and run tests. There is no need to switch tools just for the interview.
  • Practice a disciplined debugging loop: reproduce the problem, form a hypothesis, inspect the relevant code, make a focused change, and verify the result.
  • Use the 2–4-hour window deliberately. Start by getting the app running and identifying the most consequential issues; reserve time for validation and reviewing your diff.
  • Treat AI-generated changes as code you are responsible for. Check assumptions, unintended behavior changes, and whether tests actually exercise the problem.
  • Keep brief notes on what you found, what you fixed, how you verified it, and what remains. These will help you explain your decisions in the review.

Follow the assignment’s instructions for setup and submission. If access or the environment prevents you from getting started, contact your recruiting contact.

03Take-home project review30 minutes · With one of our engineers+

What to expect

You’ll walk through your solution and run the application live. Your interviewer will ask about the issues you found, the changes you made, and the tooling you used. Follow-up questions will explore whether you understand the changes the AI made—not just whether the application runs.

How to prepare

  • Start the app before the interview and confirm that the setup, dependencies, and any necessary test data work. Be ready to share your screen and demonstrate the relevant behavior.
  • Plan a short walkthrough: the issue, its root cause, your fix, and the evidence that the fix works. For performance improvements, bring measurements where available rather than relying on impressions.
  • Review your entire diff. Be able to explain changes to application code, tests, configuration, and dependencies, including anything generated by AI.
  • Be ready to discuss your AI workflow: how you directed the tool, where its suggestions were useful or wrong, and how you validated its output.
  • Explain prioritization and limitations candidly. If something is incomplete or you are uncertain about a change, say so and describe the next step you would take.

04System design interview60 minutes · Technical design discussion+

What to expect

This is a standard system design interview, similar to those at other major technology companies. You’ll work through a design problem with your interviewer and explain your reasoning as the discussion develops.

How to prepare

  • Familiarize yourself with Excalidraw, the collaborative whiteboard we use during the system design interview. Practice adding shapes, connecting them with arrows, and labeling components so you can focus on the design rather than learning the tool during the interview.
  • Practice clarifying requirements before drawing an architecture: users, core workflows, expected scale, constraints, and what is out of scope.
  • Review the fundamentals: APIs, data modeling, databases, caching, queues, consistency, reliability, and observability. Focus on when and why you would use each component.
  • Work through a practice design aloud. Start with a simple end-to-end solution, then go deeper on the areas that matter most for the requirements.
  • Explain tradeoffs rather than naming technologies. Discuss failure modes, bottlenecks, operational complexity, security, and how the design could evolve.
  • Treat the session as a conversation. State assumptions, ask questions, and adapt when the interviewer introduces new constraints.

05Conversation with the CTOFinal conversation · Timing shared during scheduling+

What to expect

This is a bar-raising and culture-fit conversation with Beacon's CTO. We will talk about what you have built, the standards you hold for your work, and how you operate when the problem is hard and the path is unclear. It is also your chance to ask about Beacon's engineering direction and the FDE role.

How to prepare

  • Why Beacon, why FDE. Be ready to explain what draws you to forward deployed engineering, particularly the combination of hands-on engineering and working directly with business operators.
  • Show us hunger. Come with a story where you went well beyond what was asked because you cared about the outcome. We want to hear what you do when nobody is assigning you the work, and how you handled it when something went wrong along the way.
  • Bring your hardest problem and your steepest learning curve. Tell us about the hardest technical problem you have solved: why it was hard, what did not work, and what kept you going. Then tell us about a time you had to get good at something unfamiliar fast, how you did it, and how you connected that work to an outcome someone cared about.
  • Have a point of view and bring questions. Share where AI-assisted engineering makes you more effective, where it needs supervision, and how you maintain quality. Ask us how deployments are chosen, what strong performance looks like, and how FDEs collaborate with the core engineering team.

ONE LAST CHECK

Before your interviews

  • Read about Beacon, our values, and the FDE role, and note what you want to learn from the team.
  • Confirm the time zone, meeting details, and any stage-specific instructions.
  • Please be ready to start on time. Joining a few minutes early gives you time to check your connection and helps us cover the full interview, including your questions, without running over the scheduled time.
  • For the project review, check screen sharing and run the app locally beforehand.
  • Ask your recruiting contact about any access issues, accommodations, or scheduling needs.

The best preparation is knowing your own work well, making your reasoning clear, and being candid about what you know and what you would investigate next.
